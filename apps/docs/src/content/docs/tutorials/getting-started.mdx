This tutorial takes you from an empty browser tab to a **solved, EC3-verified portal
frame with a signed-ready A4 calculation note** in about five minutes. Everything runs
locally: the Rust solver compiled to WebAssembly executes inside your tab, and no
project data ever leaves your machine.

## Step 1 — Open the studio and load a template

Open the studio at `rdm-studio-app.vercel.app`. The app is a progressive web app: the
first load caches the solver, the EN 10365 / EN 10210 / EN 10056 profile catalogs and
the print fonts, so every later start works fully offline. From the gallery
(gallery icon in the 48 px header) or the hero launch button, pick the
**Portique 18 m** template. The model opens with two HEA 200 columns, an IPE 300
girder, an encastré-articulé support layout and a gravity load case already wired to an
EN 1990 combination.

If you prefer starting from a blank canvas, skip the gallery and draw with the
`Créer une poutre` tool: click two grid points (magnetic snap pulls to the 50 mm grid
by default), then pick a section from the IPE catalog in the inspector.

## Step 2 — Inspect geometry and sections

At the **Géométrie** step the left inspector lists every member with its profile,
length and mass. Click the girder and change its section to `IPE 330` to feel the
instant recalculation: the model mass, the EC3 rate badge and the 3D silhouette update
without any reload. The property rows use JetBrains Mono tabular figures, so numbers
stay aligned while you compare candidates.

## Step 3 — Check supports and loads

Move to **Liaisons** and **Charges**. The template ships a pinned base on one column
and a fixed base on the other (with anti-roll restraint — every collinear X-frame
locks `Rx` on at least one support). The load case is a uniform gravity load
`{ x: 0, y: 0, z: -1 }` attached to the girder, aggregated into combination
`1.35·G + 1.50·Q` per EN 1990 6.10. Open the combination selector to see the recipe;
switching to `6.10a/b` re-weights the actions with the recommended ψ factors.

## Step 4 — Run the solver and read the results

Press **Calculer**. The Cholesky LDLᵀ factorization solves the 6-DOF-per-node system
in under 20 ms for this size of model, and the **Calcul** step renders the deformed
shape with the 5-tier thermal spectrum, the `My / Vz / N` uPlot diagrams and the EC3
verification rates per member. Scrub the cutting plane from `0` to `1,00 L` to see the
synchronized 2D Von Mises section cut at any position.

## Step 5 — Export the calculation note

At **Rapport**, the note assembles itself: cover with firm branding (Pro edition),
reactions table, Eurocode verification summary, deflection checks against `L/250` and
`L/300`, and the SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact model that was solved. Generate the
vector A4 PDF (Takumi renderer with embedded fonts) or the structured `.docx`, or copy
any table as TSV for a spreadsheet.

## What you learned

- The 5-step workspace carries a model from geometry to a signed-ready note.
- Templates open fully loaded — supports, loads and combinations included.
- The solver runs locally in milliseconds; the thermal spectrum and uPlot diagrams
  make results readable at a glance.
- Every number in the report traces back to a normative clause through CalcTrace.
