A transport or lifting cradle sees gravity **plus** the accelerations of the lifting
gear, the road, or the sea. This tutorial builds a two-transverse cradle for a 12 t
colis, applies the dynamic field `(ax, ay, az) × g = (0,4 ; 0,4 ; 1,3) × g`, and
verifies the transverses in S355 steel — then repeats the check in aluminium 6082-T6
under EN 1999.

## Step 1 — Draw the cradle skeleton

Start from a blank project. The cradle is two parallel IPE 200 transverses
(`L = 2,40 m`) sitting on longitudinal runners. Draw with the beam tool on the 50 mm
grid, or import the wireframe from a DXF (see the CAD guide). Set the units panel to
metres, keep the Z-up repère, and use `Ctrl+D` to duplicate the first transverse at a
2,00 m spacing.

```text
N1 (0, 0, 0)      N2 (2.4, 0, 0)   — transverse A
N5 (0, 2.0, 0)    N6 (2.4, 2.0, 0) — transverse B
B1..B2 transverses IPE 200 · S355J2
```

## Step 2 — Apply the cargo mass and the acceleration field

Instead of guessing an equivalent static force, attach the cargo as a **mass** at its
four bearing points (1 t each, via the mass tool) and let the solver integrate the
acceleration field. In the **Charges** step, open
**Champs d'accélération (g)** and enter:

```yaml
acc_field:
  ax: 0.4   # traction / braking (×g)
  ay: 0.4   # racking / rolling (×g)
  az: 1.3   # lifting dynamic amplification (×g)
```

The field becomes an inertial body load on every member and every attached mass — the
cradle's own steel weight is amplified exactly like the cargo, with no fictitious
equivalent point loads to maintain. Each acceleration component is registered as its
own action so EN 1990 combinations can weight them independently.

## Step 3 — Combine per EN 1990

Open the combination table. The generators create the fundamental combinations:

```text
ELU-1 : G + 1,3·g_z + 0,4·g_y   (levage + roulis)
ELU-2 : G + 1,3·g_z − 0,4·g_y   (levage + roulis inversé)
ELS-c : G + 1,0·g_z
```

Sign inversion matters: a racking acceleration that stabilizes the cargo in one
combination destabilizes it in the mirrored one. RDM Studio generates both gisements
automatically, and `6.10a/b` re-weights with ψ₀ when you switch the recipe selector.

## Step 4 — Verify the transverses

Solve. The verification panel shows, per member and per combination, the flexion rate
`MEd / Mc,Rd` (EN 1993-1-1 §6.2.5), the shear rate against `Vpl,Rd` (§6.2.6) and —
for the S355 transverse — the class check `c/t ≤ 33ε` on the flange. For the worked
example the governing ELU gives `MEd = 45,9 kN·m` against
`Mc,Rd = 78,3 kN·m` → η = 0,59.

## Step 5 — Swap to aluminium 6082-T6 and compare

Duplicate the project, switch the transverse material to **Aluminium 6082-T6**
(EN 1999-1-1: `fo = 250 MPa`, `fu = 290 MPa`). The solver re-runs with the EN 1999
verification chain — including the welded-zone factors if you declare welds — and the
comparison table shows the mass saving against the steel design. Export the PDF note
with both materials side by side; the SHA-256 fingerprint ties the note to the exact
model state.

## What you learned

- Acceleration fields convert dynamic transport environments into EN 1990 actions
  without fictitious loads.
- Both gisements of every acceleration enter the combinations automatically.
- The same geometry verifies in EN 1993 steel and EN 1999 aluminium.
